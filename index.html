Altamont Pass Wind Farm

I chose the Altamont Pass Wind Farm in California. It is one of the oldest and largest wind energy areas in the United States. The wind turbines produce renewable electricity, linking them to climate change solutions and green technology. Still, they are also a major issue, as they are among the leading causes of bird deaths. Located within Northern California in the Altamont Pass of the Diablo Range, it covers large dry hills and grassland landscapes. Known to be one of the earliest wind farms, built in the early 1980s. It produces around 125 MW on average and 1.1 terawatt-hours (TWh) yearly.




Unfortunately, although wind farms provide benefits like renewable energy and reduce the need for fossil fuels, animals like golden eagles and hawks are at risk of being killed by colliding with the rotating blades. Golden eagles are federally protected birds, and in the year 2007 alone, 70 eagles were killed among the 1300 bird casualties, with a total of 4700 bird deaths annually.





Smaller turbines would also create a lot of noise, which would scare away animals and become disruptive to both the animals and residents who live near the farms. There have been many reports of noise complaints regarding the turbines, taking up to three decades of lawsuits before they agreed to a change. They also paid up to $2.5 million for raptor habitat restoration to make up for the bird deaths over the years.
